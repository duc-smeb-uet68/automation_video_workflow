# Research notes: The Missing Hour on Every Recording

The narration is original fiction. Bellwether, its residents, June's disappearance, the missing recordings, and all evidence in the case are invented. The sources below informed details and investigative questions; they do not report or explain the fictional event.

- [NIST, Daylight Saving Time Rules](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/popular-links/daylight-saving-time-dst): In most of the United States, spring clock changes skip from 2 a.m. to 3 a.m. NIST time services transmit UTC; local time and daylight-saving adjustments depend on system settings. This informed the distinction between a missing clock label and missing recorded events. A clock change does not explain the story's imagined gaps.
- [Lacy and Stark, The Neuroscience of Memory: Implications for the Courtroom](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4183265/), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2013: A review describes memory distortion, reconstructive processes, and the influence of later information and questioning. This informed separate witness interviews and caution around recalled details. It does not establish a mechanism for a town-wide amnesia event.
- [NIST, Digital and Multimedia Evidence](https://www.nist.gov/spo/forensic-science-program/forensic-science-research/digital-and-multimedia-evidence): NIST describes testing and guidance for examining digital evidence, including the limits of forensic tools. This informed the investigator's attention to original devices, provenance, and multiple possible causes. The fictional findings are not a forensic case study.
- [National Archives, Watergate: The 18½ Minute Gap and Haldeman's Notes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJDX7AFujYs): The National Archives describes forensic work concerning a real gap in Nixon White House recordings and related notes. This informed the general theme of incomplete recordings. It is a different historical event and provides no evidence for the story's incident.

Research accessed 2026-11-02. Sources are background for creative choices, not citations for fictional facts. The National Archives page on tape preservation returned a 502 response, so the accessible National Archives video description was used for the historical background instead.
