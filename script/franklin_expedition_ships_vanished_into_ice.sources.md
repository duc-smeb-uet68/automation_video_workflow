# Research notes: Franklin expedition

- [Canadian Museum of History: Victory Point note](https://www.historymuseum.ca/blog/a-very-special-piece-of-paper) — transcript, 1847 and 1848 entries, deaths, abandonment, and Back River plan.
- [Parks Canada: Expedition history](https://www.parcs.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nu/epaveswrecks/culture/histoire-history) — voyage, Inuit knowledge, and discovery context.
- [Parks Canada: Finding HMS Terror](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nu/epaveswrecks/culture/archeologie-archeology/decouvertes-discoveries/terror) — 2016 discovery, Sammy Kogvik's information, identification, and wreck condition.
- [Parks Canada: Discoveries](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nu/epaveswrecks/culture/archeologie-archeology/decouvertes-discoveries) — Erebus discovered in 2014 and Terror in 2016.
- [Smithsonian Magazine: Franklin expedition and Inuit testimony](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-shipwrecks-from-john-franklins-doomed-arctic-expedition-were-exactly-where-the-inuit-said-they-would-be-180986627/) — expedition chronology, Inuit testimony, archaeological evidence, disputed explanations, and ongoing research.
- [University of Waterloo: Identification of James Fitzjames](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/media/another-franklin-expedition-crew-member-has-been-identified) — DNA identification and cut marks.

Narration distinguishes the documented 1848 plan from unconfirmed later movements. Lead poisoning, the cause of Franklin's death, and the final route of the ships remain uncertain. Runtime depends on narration speed and pauses.
