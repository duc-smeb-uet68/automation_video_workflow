Every night at 11:47, the last train arrived at a station that did not exist.

That was what Mara's grandfather used to say. He had worked on the railway for forty years, and he kept one strange ticket locked inside his desk. It was yellow with age, stamped with tomorrow's date, and printed with a destination: Bellwether.

When he died, Mara found the ticket beside a note: "If the train comes, do not miss it."

At 11:47 the next night, she stood alone on the platform. The departure board went dark. A train slid out of the tunnel without a sound, its windows glowing amber. The doors opened, and Mara stepped inside.

Each carriage held things people had lost: a child's red mitten, a silver key, letters never sent. In the final carriage, she found a blue music box. Her grandfather had given it to her when she was six. She had lost it when her family moved away.

A conductor appeared. He said the railway collected things people still needed to find. Each passenger could take only one.

Mara picked up the music box. Then she noticed her grandfather's old cap beside it. She reached for that too, but the conductor shook his head.

Mara wound the music box. A familiar melody filled the carriage, and she remembered her grandfather humming it as he packed her toys. She smiled, set the box down, and took the cap.

At dawn, she woke on the station bench. The ticket was gone. The cap was in her lap, and tucked inside was a photograph of her grandfather holding the music box, smiling at someone just outside the frame.
